# VSL script writing

Two sources, merged:
- **Brunson's Hook, Story, Offer** (original kept in `brunson-original.md`, with its discovery questions, S/A/B/D tier tactics and templates).
- **The 10 tips** from a VSL practitioner (transcript summarized below).

**DUXIO override.** For DUXIO, `duxio-brand` and `duxio-vsl` win on every conflict. Specifically ignore these generic tactics for DUXIO: price reveal and offer stacking in the video, fake scarcity or "only X spots", guarantees, bonuses, "free" anything, and revealing price. The CTA is "Book a Strategy Call". No dashes, no income gate, no invented proof.

## The 10 tips (the structure of a high converting VSL)
1. **Talk about the problem first.** Open with the viewer's problem in their own words, as questions they would answer "yes, that's me" to. Do not jump to your solution. Use phrases real leads have said to you. This builds trust before any pitch.
2. **Talk about THE solution, not YOUR solution (yet).** Teach the steps anyone could take to fix the problem, whether or not they hire you (1, 3 or 5 steps). One of the steps is the thing you sell, written as "you need X, here is how", never "we can do X for you". This reads as value, not as a pitch.
3. **Address the elephant in the room.** Name the alternatives viewers are silently comparing you to (a PDF guide, a website, an assistant, doing it themselves) and show the difference. Each answered alternative removes one objection.
4. **Use "it's kind of like".** One or two simple analogies for the hard concepts ("it's kind of like a salesperson that works 24/7").
5. **Transition smoothly into your offer.** "At this point you're probably thinking, this sounds great, but how do I get it done?" Then give two options: the hard way (do it yourself: more time, more mistakes, weaker results) and the easy way (we do it for you). The call to action comes right after the easy way is described.
6. **Presentation style format.** Slides with text that changes every 2 to 3 seconds to mirror the voiceover (100 to 200 slides, not a few bullet slides). Optional: a small circle with the speaker in a corner, and B-roll to mix things up. Beats talking head only and whiteboard animation.
7. **Length: 5 to 10 minutes is the sweet spot.** Under 2 minutes can't carry enough value. Too long with fluff loses people. Exception: if the VSL converts so well that you can't handle the calls, make a longer, more detailed version to filter and attract more ready buyers. Shorter first, longer only once the short one works.
8. **Read the analytics.** After 50 to 100 viewers, target about 80% watching the first minute, a gradual decline, and about 25% reaching the end. Any big drop off point: change or cut what is said there. Repeat every version.
9. **Listen to lead feedback.** On the calls, note which parts people quote back (keep those) and which confused them (fix those). Real conversations beat the raw data.
10. **Get an expert to review.** Fresh eyes from someone who has reviewed many VSLs avoid the common mistakes and speed up results.

## Process (use this every time)
1. **Discovery.** Ask the missing questions from `brunson-original.md` (product, audience, goal, big promise, unique mechanism, objections, tone). For DUXIO these are answered by `brand/duxio/`; don't re-ask.
2. **Structure** (merged):
   - Hook (first 10 seconds): pattern interrupt + the problem the viewer feels + the promise to explain.
   - Problem in their language (tip 1), with three levels of pain: external, internal, philosophical.
   - Story / epiphany bridge, short, real, never invented.
   - The solution steps anyone can take (tip 2), with alternatives addressed (tip 3) and analogies (tip 4).
   - Two options transition (tip 5), then the offer and one clear CTA.
   - Future pacing: the viewer's life after, in one or two lines.
3. **Format** (tip 6) and **length** (tip 7). Slide cadence of 2 to 3 seconds, voiceover word for word.
4. **Write simple.** Read it aloud. Cut anything that delays the point. Run `copy-editing` and the AI tell check.
5. **After publishing:** tips 8 and 9 define the iteration loop. Log what changed per version.

## Pre-flight
- Problem before solution; the first minute earns the second.
- The solution section teaches without selling.
- Every alternative the viewer is thinking about is answered.
- One CTA, said and shown.
- No invented proof, price games, fake urgency or guarantees.
- For DUXIO: the `duxio-brand` §9 pre-flight passes.
