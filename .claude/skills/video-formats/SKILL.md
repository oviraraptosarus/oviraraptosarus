# Video formats

Pick the format from the goal (what should the viewer feel, know, or do afterwards?), then use its playbook. Anything not listed: use the closest format and adapt. Quality rules from `video-craft` apply to all of them.

## Universal specs
| Channel | Canvas | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reels, TikTok, Shorts, Stories | 1080×1920 (9:16) | keep text inside the centre 900×1400; clear the bottom ~320px, top ~120px, right ~120px |
| Feed (IG/LinkedIn/FB) | 1080×1350 (4:5) or 1080×1080 | 4:5 gets more screen |
| YouTube, website, VSL, webinars | 1920×1080 (16:9) | 30fps (60 only for screen capture) |
| Export | H.264 + AAC | about -14 LUFS for social, about -16 LUFS for YouTube/web, true peak under -1 dBTP |

Voiceover speed: persuasive about 2.5 words/s, reel about 3, calm explainer about 2.2. Captions on everything for social (most watch muted): 4–7 words per line, word-synced highlight.

## 1. Reel / Short (organic, 7–45s, best 15–30s)
Goal: retention and shares. One idea only. Structure: hook (0–1.5s, strongest visual and a line already on screen at frame 0, no fade-up) → promise → body in beats with a change every 2–4s → payoff → loop or one-line CTA. Cuts uneven (momentum, not a metronome). Captions always. A sentence that feeds back into the hook, or a matched first and last frame, gives a loop.

## 2. Paid ad (15–30s; test 6–15s cutdowns)
Goal: a measurable action. Structure: hook (3s: pain, claim or pattern break) → problem/desire → product as the answer, shown in use → proof (number, testimonial, demo) → offer → CTA with a specific verb. Brand visible by about 2s but not as a logo intro. Sound-off safe. Produce 3 hook variants and 2 lengths when asked for "an ad". Claims must be provable from supplied material.

## 3. Promo / launch (30–60s)
Goal: awareness plus desire for a product, event or release. Structure: tease → what it is in one line → three standout moments (each a distinct visual idea) → proof or social signal → date/offer → CTA. Higher energy, bigger music moments; the logo lands on the final hit.

## 4. VSL: video sales letter (2–10+ min, 16:9)
Goal: sell through one persuasive narrative. Calm confident pacing, text supports the voice and never competes with it, and every key line holds 1.5–2s. Structure: hook (the big promise or pain, 0–15s) → problem → agitation (cost of inaction, a short story) → discovery of the solution/mechanism → how it works (one scene per step, offer or funnel, drawn as real diagrams and UI) → proof (results, testimonials, only real ones) → the offer stack and price framing → guarantee → objection handling → urgency (only if real) → CTA repeated 2–3 times. Script first (about 150 wpm), then storyboard, then build. Visual vocabulary: typography-led, diagrams drawing on, UI assembling, counters, checklists; no gimmick effects.

## 5. Explainer (60–120s)
Goal: understanding. Structure: relatable problem → simple mental model (one metaphor, kept throughout) → how it works in 3 steps → result → CTA. One new idea per beat; diagrams build progressively; consistent iconography.

## 6. Product demo / tutorial (30s–5min)
Goal: show it working. Structure: outcome first → steps in the order the user does them → result. Real UI captured or accurately rebuilt, with cursor, click and state changes, and callouts that appear exactly when mentioned. Chapters for long ones. Never fake a result.

## 7. Testimonial / case study (30–90s)
Goal: trust. Structure: who they are and the before → the trigger → what changed → the measurable result → quote. The result number is the hero. Only real data and quotes from supplied material. Edit the speaker's words tightly, with lower third, captions and light B-roll.

## 8. Talking-head long-form (YouTube, 3–20min) with B-roll
Goal: keep a watcher through the whole talk. Cut dead air, filler and retakes. Hook in the first 15s with a preview of the payoff. A visual change at least every 5–8s: B-roll, graphic, overlay, or a reframe. B-roll illustrates the exact words being said (see `footage-edit`). Chapter cards, lower thirds, captions, a quiet music bed carved under the voice, and an end screen CTA.

## 9. Clips from podcasts / webinars (20–60s each)
Goal: reach. Find self-contained moments (a claim, a story, a contrarian take). Hook line on screen at frame 0. Vertical reframe with speaker tracking, animated captions, a progress bar, minimal B-roll. Title card with the takeaway.

## 10. Stories / countdowns / teasers (5–15s)
One message, one action. Big type, one hero visual, a clear ending. Countdowns show the number as the hero and the date and action beneath.

## 11. Brand pieces: logo sting, lower thirds, intro/outro, title cards (2–6s)
Reusable, templated for many names or titles. Match the brand's motion language exactly; keep the lockup aligned and the exit as clean as the entrance.

## Brief to infer before building (never interview if it can be inferred)
Goal and action wanted → audience → single message → tone register → format and channel → length → source material (site, script, footage, brand colours and fonts) → what proof exists. If the source is a website, extract the offer, funnel steps, proof, brand colours and fonts from it and use only what's there.
